--- IMPORTANT VERIFIED FINDING ---
Plain -fsanitize=undefined on this toolchain (GCC 13.3.0, Ubuntu 24.04)
caught NOTHING in this program - the runtime output is byte-for-byte
identical to the no-flags build. This was double-checked directly:

  $ gcc -g -fsanitize=float-cast-overflow src/vulnerable.c -o v3b_floatcast
  $ ./v3b_floatcast
  src/vulnerable.c:63:9: runtime error: 1e+10 is outside the range of
  representable values of type 'int'

So the float -> int conversion of 1.0e10f IS genuine undefined behaviour
(the C standard leaves an out-of-range float-to-int conversion
undefined) and CAN be caught by a sanitizer check - but on GCC,
float-cast-overflow is a separate check that is NOT pulled in by the
"undefined" umbrella group the way it is on Clang. It has to be
requested explicitly with -fsanitize=float-cast-overflow.

None of the other three bugs (short truncation, signed->unsigned
conversion, atoi() misparsing) are undefined behaviour at all under the
C standard - they are well-defined (implementation-defined or fully
defined) conversions that simply produce a mathematically wrong answer.
UBSan is designed to catch undefined behaviour, not "well-defined but
logically wrong" conversions, so by design it has nothing to say about
them regardless of which UBSan sub-checks are enabled.

--- ANALYSIS ---
UBSan is powerful for catching a narrow, specific class of defects
(signed integer overflow, invalid shifts, null-pointer misuse,
out-of-range float-to-int casts, etc.) but only the sub-checks that are
actually enabled. On GCC, -fsanitize=undefined is not a complete
superset of every individual UBSan check; float-cast-overflow in
particular must be requested explicitly. Even with the right check
enabled, UBSan still cannot flag conversions that are well-defined by
the standard but semantically wrong for the program (e.g. -1 becoming
4294967295), because "well-defined" and "correct" are not the same
thing.
